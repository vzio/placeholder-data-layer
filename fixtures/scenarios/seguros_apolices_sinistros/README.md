# seguros_apolices_sinistros

A motor insurer: `segurados` (320), `veiculos` (499), `apolices` (499) and `sinistros` (450).
Generated by `seguros_apolices_sinistros` in the people pack; `preview.json` shows the first rows.

What the data holds:

- **Plates** in four layouts: old with a hyphen (`ABC-1234`), old without (`ABC1234`), Mercosul
  (`ABC1D23`), and old plates converted to Mercosul (`ABC1234` to `ABC1C34`).
- **RENAVAM** valid by its mod-11 rule, held in an **integer** column, so registrations from the
  nine-digit era lose their leading zeros (143 of 499 print nine digits).
- **Chassis numbers**, seventeen characters with a Brazilian manufacturer prefix, never `I`, `O`
  or `Q`.
- **Driving licence numbers**, eleven bare digits, none a valid CPF.
- **Claims** with heavy-tailed payouts, police report numbers (`BO 1234/2024 - 5º DP`), and for
  collisions with a third party, that party's plate, name and phone in their own columns and
  repeated in the narrative.

What the engine does today: the licence number is classified as a phone, so it keeps its first
three digits and two rows gain CPF validity. RENAVAM and the manufacture year are integers and
are jittered like quantities: a RENAVAM keeps its magnitude and leading digits, and years reach
1950 and 2106. Plates, chassis and policy numbers are redacted to masks (`XXX0X00`), keeping the
plate layouts but collapsing every plate into three values. Payouts are jittered with outliers in
the hundreds of billions.

Not expressible today: RENAVAM check-digit validity (digit count stands in), and the plate in the
narrative is compared by its digits, since a plate's three letters are too short to be a
meaningful fragment.
