# assinaturas_saas

A SaaS billing database: `contas` (250 customer companies), `usuarios` (their users),
`assinaturas` (one subscription per account, two when an older one was cancelled) and `faturas`
(one invoice per billing cycle). Generated by `assinaturas_saas` in the commerce pack;
`preview.json` shows the first rows of each table.

What it is about is **global identity**. Every row has a public UUID, the id the product shows
in URLs, webhooks and support tickets, and child tables repeat their parents' public ids next to
the integer foreign keys (`usuarios.conta_public_id`, `faturas.assinatura_public_id`,
`faturas.conta_public_id`). An account's UUID therefore appears in four tables with no declared
foreign key between them; it must come out as one new UUID everywhere it appears, and two
accounts must never collapse into one.

Also in the data:

- **Logins** (`usuarios.login`) built from the user's name the way people choose them
  (`maria.souza`, `msouza`, `souza.maria`, `maria88`), unique across the dataset.
- **Corporate emails**: most users share their account's domain; some signed up with a free
  provider.
- **Accounts** with a masked CNPJ; about one in ten is a sole trader whose legal name ends in the
  owner's CPF.
- **Prices** from a public list of four plans, monthly or annual (ten months' price); invoices
  repeat their subscription's price. Trials have no invoice; half the refunded invoices have no
  service-invoice number.
- `ultimo_acesso` is a `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS` timestamp, NULL for users who never logged in.

What the vocabulary cannot yet say, kept here as prose:

- An invoice's `valor` equals its subscription's `valor_ciclo`. Keeping that join-level
  equality needs an assertion comparing a column with a column of the row its foreign key joins.
- A user's email domain equals their account's. The same join-level comparison, on the email
  domain, would pin it.
