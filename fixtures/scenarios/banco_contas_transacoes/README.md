# banco_contas_transacoes

A digital bank: `clientes` (350), `contas` (520), `chaves_pix` (783) and `transacoes` (2500).
Generated by `banco_contas_transacoes` in the people pack; `preview.json` shows the first rows.

What the data holds:

- **Customers**, individual (CPF) and business (CNPJ), in one masked `documento` column. Sole
  traders' legal names embed the owner's CPF, as the MEI convention has it.
- **PIX keys** in one column, stored as the directory stores them: CPF and CNPJ as bare digits,
  emails as typed, phones in E.164 (`+5511987654321`), and random keys as lowercase UUIDs. Each
  key belongs to its account holder, and each kind of document, email or phone key is registered
  once per customer.
- **Balances** heavy-tailed over five decades, with some overdrafts.
- **Transactions** whose amounts run from cents to tens of thousands of reais. PIX transfers
  name the counterparty (`PIX enviado para Ana S`) and, when sent, the destination key; over half
  of those keys are registered in `chaves_pix`, so a transfer resolves to an account. Five
  transactions reference an account that no longer exists.

What the engine does today: the mixed `documento` column is classified CPF, so CNPJ rows come
back as CPFs and lose both layout and validity. The PIX-key column is free text and redacted,
which keeps the zero-masked CPF and CNPJ keys' digit layout but turns phones into
`+0000000000000`, UUIDs into `00000xx0-...` and emails into `xxxxx@xxxxx.xxx`, so the key kind,
the domains and the area codes are lost and over half the keys collide. Amounts and balances are
jittered with outliers in the hundreds of billions, and an overdraft's sign is not guaranteed.
