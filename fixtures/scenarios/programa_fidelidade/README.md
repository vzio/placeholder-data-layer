# programa_fidelidade

A loyalty programme: `membros` (600) and `lancamentos` (the points ledger, about 2000 entries).
Generated by `programa_fidelidade` in the commerce pack; `preview.json` shows the first rows of
each table.

- **Members** have a CPF (masked or bare digits, a few NULL), email, phone, tier (`bronze` most
  often, `diamante` rarely) and join date.
- **Birth dates** in ISO and Brazilian layouts, a few NULL and a few impossible (`2023-02-30`,
  `00/00/0000`).
- **Linked card**: just over half the members have one, stored as its last four digits behind a
  mask, in the short (`**** 1234`) or the four-group (`**** **** **** 1234`) layout.
- **A heavy-tailed ledger.** A member's entry count is a small base plus a scale divided by a
  uniform rank, so most members have one to three entries and a handful have around a hundred.
  Entries are shuffled, as a ledger ordered by time would interleave them. Each is an accrual
  (positive points, `Compra loja 0231`), a redemption (the voucher's cost, negative), an expiry
  or a manual adjustment.

The fan-out is what a sandbox most often gets wrong: a member with a hundred entries must
still have a hundred, and the members who had one must still have one. `key_identity` on
`membros.id` and `lancamentos.membro_id` pins exactly that, since equal keys staying equal and
distinct keys staying distinct preserves every member's count.

What the vocabulary cannot yet say, kept here as prose:

- A birth date's year (and so the age distribution) should survive within a year or two. A
  `distribution` feature on a date's year, or an age band, would pin it; today only the layout
  mix is checked.
- Each member's balance (the sum of their entries) should be what it was. An aggregate-per-key
  assertion would check it directly; `points_are_unchanged` is the stronger row-level wish.
