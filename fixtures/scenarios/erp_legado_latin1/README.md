# erp_legado_latin1

The customer master (`cad_cliente`, 1200) and receivables (`mov_titulo`, 3000) of a terminal-era
ERP, joined by an integer customer code with no declared constraint, so nine receivables point at
customers that were deleted. Generated by `erp_legado_latin1` in the legacy pack; `preview.json`
shows the first rows.

The mess of a system older than Unicode:

- **Upper case and fixed width.** Names are `CHAR(40)`, addresses `CHAR(50)`, neighbourhoods and
  cities `CHAR(30)`, phones `CHAR(15)`: every value right-padded with spaces, and cut when too long.
- **Mojibake.** The first 40% of customers were migrated from the previous system, whose UTF-8
  bytes were read back as Windows-1252: `SÃƒO PAULO`, `CONCEIÃ‡ÃƒO`, `Ã\x81GUA VERDE`. Later
  customers were keyed in folded to ASCII (`SAO PAULO`).
- **Numbers that lost their zeros.** The CPF is a `BIGINT`: a CPF starting with `0` prints with
  ten digits or fewer. So is the CEP: every São Paulo CEP (`01310-100`) is a seven-digit integer.
- **Integer dates.** Birth, registration, issue and due dates are `YYYYMMDD` integers; `0` and
  `19000101` mean "unknown".
- **Sentinels.** `0` in the CPF and CEP; `NULL`, `N/A`, `-` and `0` typed as text into the phone
  and email columns; real NULLs elsewhere.
- **Old phone layout** `(011)3265-4321` with the trunk zero, beside bare digits.
- **Remarks** in upper case, some repeating the customer's CPF or phone as bare digits.

What the scenario asks: identifiers vanish (the integer CPF included, which the engine does find),
and everything that makes the dump look like this ERP survives — the integer shapes, the
sentinels, the fixed width and the upper case, the mojibake rate, valid `YYYYMMDD` dates, the
two-value flags and statuses.

Open rulings: whether a `0` CPF counts as a malformed document and as a test-row signal (the
engine counts it as both, which also makes every one of those 72 rows "mixed"), whether cities
and states pass through, and the tolerance on receivable amounts.

Not expressible today, so stated here: the due date should stay after the issue date in every
row (a cross-column invariant); a sentinel should come back as the same sentinel rather than any
value of its shape (`format_preserved` with a one-value regex is the closest form and is used).
