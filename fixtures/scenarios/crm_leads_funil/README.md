# crm_leads_funil

A CRM lead funnel: `leads` (1560) and `interacoes` (the sales team's touches on each lead).
Generated by `crm_leads_funil` in the commerce pack; `preview.json` shows the first rows of each
table.

What a lead table looks like when it is filled by web forms, ad integrations and event badges:

- **Sparse.** A name is missing on about one lead in eight and is only a first name on another
  fifth; email is missing on about one in six, phone on nearly half, company on half, job title
  more often still. Every NULL is meaningful: it is what the form did not ask.
- **Two kinds of email.** A lead with a company has, more often than not, a corporate address
  built from the company's name (`ana.souza@aurorametalurgica.com.br`); the rest use a free
  provider.
- **Skewed source enum.** `origem` is half `organico`, then `google_ads`, `meta_ads`, `indicacao`,
  `linkedin`, `evento`, `parceiro`. UTM fields follow it: organic leads carry none, paid ones all
  three, referral and partner leads a source and a medium but no campaign.
- **Consent.** `consentimento_marketing` is true, false or NULL; `data_consentimento` is present
  exactly when consent is true.
- **Duplicates.** Sixty people filled in a form a second time, often with the email in capitals
  and the phone left out.
- **Interactions** have a kind, a date and a summary that is usually empty or plain, sometimes
  repeating the lead's phone or full name.

The scenario asks for identifiers to vanish while the shape of the funnel survives exactly:
every NULL in place, consent flags and attribution untouched, enums with their exact mix.

What the vocabulary cannot yet say, kept here as prose:

- `data_consentimento` is present if and only if `consentimento_marketing` is true. That holds
  afterwards only indirectly, through each column's null positions and the flags being
  unchanged; a conditional assertion ("null exactly where that column is not true") would pin
  the dependency itself, whatever the engine does to either column.
- An interaction summary that names its lead holds a name that lives in `leads`; a
  letter-fragment check across a foreign key would catch it surviving.
