# pdv_lojas_fisicas

Point-of-sale records of a chain of physical stores: `lojas` (30), `operadores` (the cashiers)
and `vendas` (3000 consumer receipts). Generated by `pdv_lojas_fisicas` in the commerce pack;
`preview.json` shows the first rows of each table.

- **Stores.** Most are branches of one company and share its CNPJ root, differing only in the
  branch number (`/0001-`, `/0002-`, …) and check digits; about one in five is a franchise with a
  CNPJ of its own. Each has a city, state and CEP.
- **Cashiers** have a staff number (`OP` followed by the store and a sequence), a name and a
  CPF.
- **Sales** are concentrated on the first, flagship stores. Each carries:
  - the **NFC-e access key**, 44 digits: the state's IBGE code, the year and month, the issuing
    store's CNPJ, model `65`, series, receipt number, emission type, a random code and a mod-11
    check digit. The store's CNPJ is inside every key it issued.
  - an optional **`cpf_na_nota`**, the CPF a customer asks to have printed on the receipt,
    present on about three sales in ten, masked or bare. The CPFs come from 250 returning
    customers, a few of whom ask on dozens of receipts.
  - a timestamp, a receipt number, a total and a payment method.

The scenario asks for the customers' and cashiers' CPFs to vanish while each returning customer
stays one customer, for the access key to stay a well-formed key, and for the store side
(CNPJs, cities, revenue) to stay true.

What the vocabulary cannot yet say, kept here as prose:

- The 14 digits at positions 7–20 of every access key equal the issuing store's CNPJ, and the
  last digit is the key's mod-11 check digit. Whatever happens to `lojas.cnpj`, the key should
  follow it, and stay valid. That needs an assertion comparing part of a value with a column of
  the row its foreign key joins, and a validity check for access keys alongside CPF and CNPJ.
- Branches share a CNPJ root; whether a redrawn chain keeps one shared root is a join-free but
  cross-row property no assertion expresses.
