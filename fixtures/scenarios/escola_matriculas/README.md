# escola_matriculas

A school's enrolment system: `turmas` (24), `responsaveis` (280), `alunos` (543), `matriculas`
(543) and `notas` (4344). Generated by `escola_matriculas` in the people pack; `preview.json`
shows the first rows.

What the data holds:

- **Minors** born 2007 to 2019, named with a first name and their guardian's surname, so
  siblings share a guardian and a surname. 428 of 543 have no CPF.
- **Guardians** with CPF, phone and email; three rows are entirely test placeholders
  (`Fulano de Tal`, `teste@teste.com`, `111.444.777-35`, `(00) 00000-0000`).
- An optional **second guardian** (a nullable foreign key onto the same table).
- **Special educational needs** for one pupil in eleven: health data about a minor, sensitive
  under LGPD art. 11 and art. 14.
- A twelve-digit **student registration number** with a check character, unique per pupil.
- **Grades** from 0 to 10 with one decimal (81 distinct values) and absence counts that are
  mostly zero, per subject and bimester.

What the engine does today: a mostly-null CPF column is still classified CPF, nulls stay in
place and valid CPFs stay valid. The registration number is redacted to one mask, so it is no
longer unique. Grades, bimester numbers and absences are jittered like measurements: grades
carry long fractions and leave the one-decimal scale, bimesters change, absences reach the tens
of millions. Subject names and class labels are redacted. Birth dates are jittered by days to
weeks, which can move a pupil across a school-year boundary.

Not expressible today: that a grade stays within 0 to 10 (no range assertion; distinct count and
printed length stand in) and that a pupil keeps their age band.
